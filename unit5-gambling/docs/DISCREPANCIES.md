# Discrepancies and open items from the curriculum audit

Things worth a second look before the next class. None blocks use of the assessment.

| # | Where | What we found | How the assessment handles it | Suggested action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brain Lab packet, **Station 3 “Roll & Risk”** | Payouts (2–5: 0×, 6–8: 1×, 9–10: 2×, 11: 3×, 12: 5×) give an expected return of 41/36 ≈ **1.14× the wager**, so the **player** has the edge, while the lesson message is “the house always wins.” | The assessment does not reuse that table as a claim about real gambling | Change a payout (for example 6–8 returns ½×) or discuss why this station is the exception |
| 2 | Brain Lab packet, Stations 1 and 4 | Station 1 is a fair game (expected value 0 per spin); Station 4 loses 1/6 token per roll on average. Different stations teach different things | Chapter 3 items use the exact payout tables from the packet | None, but be ready to explain why Station 1 “feels” rewarding even at EV 0 |
| 3 | Teen-brain timing | Slides say the prefrontal cortex finishes about the “mid-20s”; NIMH’s current page says “mid-to-late 20s” | The assessment says development continues “into the twenties” and cites NIMH | Update the slide wording if you like |
| 4 | Day 1 **Prediction Markets video** | The video was not available during the build; only the Video Guide’s questions were used | Chapter 1 *c1-market* and Chapter 4 “building a book” rely on the guide’s questions | Please review those items against the video |
| 5 | Handout statistics | “3–5% of bettors profitable” and several survey figures cite secondary sources only | Not asked as recall; one survey table is used with a self-report caveat | Add primary citations if you want students to use these figures |
| 6 | Time budget | The brief asked for about 60 minutes. The model estimates 56–66 for a typical student and up to ≈88 for a slower reader | See [BLUEPRINT.md](BLUEPRINT.md) | Pilot with one class and tell us the real distribution |
| 7 | Question count | The brief suggested about 35–45 scored interactions “adjusting for simulation complexity.” There are **32 scored questions** with **51 separately checked responses** | Fewer, richer questions so the six simulations can feed graded work inside 60 minutes | Add or remove questions in `authoring/` and rebuild |
| 8 | Lab realism | Near-miss, reward and teen-brain labs are simplified models | Labeled “simplified model” / “teaching sketch” on screen | None |
| 9 | Helpline numbers | Two numbers (1-800-GAMBLER and 1-800-522-4700) appear in the unit materials | Both are shown | Confirm they are current each year |
