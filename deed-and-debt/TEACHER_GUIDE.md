# DEED & DEBT: Teacher Guide

## What it is

Small groups (2–6 students on one Chromebook or the projector) play a property-trading board game set in the invented town of Port Juniper.

**Winning:** the winner is the player with the highest **net worth** at the end. Nobody is eliminated: a player who runs out of money sells, mortgages, or takes a bankruptcy "fresh start" and keeps playing.

**What happens each Year:**
- Every player gets a paycheck.
- A **Life/Economy Event** hits, such as a recession, a Fed rate change or a promotion.
- Some Years start with a **Ballot Measure** vote, such as a school levy, rezoning or rent control.
- At the end of the Year, every player sees an **Annual Report**: income, expenses, cash flow, debt, credit score and net worth.

**What students practice on their turns:**
- Buying, auctioning, building and trading property.
- Taking loans and building credit.
- Saving and investing, and buying insurance.
- Choosing to rent or buy a home.
- Paying taxes.
- Making tradeoff decisions, with a **Why?** button that explains each one before they commit.

**At the end,** students see:
- Charts of their game.
- Their best and toughest decisions.
- Awards.
- Reflection questions written from what actually happened in their game.

There's also a 5-question pre/post check and a printable Debrief Guide.

**Setup:** open `index.html` (or your GitHub Pages link). No accounts, no internet and no student data leave the device.

## Ways to play

- **▶ New Group Game:** 2–6 students share one device and pass it each turn. CPU players can fill empty seats.
- **🤖 Solo vs. Computer:** one student against 1–5 computer rivals (Rival Rosa, Banker Bo, Tycoon Ty, Saver Sam and Builder Bea). Each rival follows a different strategy. Solo games skip the pass-the-device screens and run about half as long, because computer turns are quick. Use them for homework, make-up work for absent students, early finishers or a 1:1 device day. Students still get the full Results page, reflections and "Copy for Google Classroom/Canvas."

**Choosing a length** (times are estimated play time, not counting about 2–3 minutes of career picks):

| Length | Years | Group of 4 | Solo | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sprint | 3 | ~14 min | ~8 min | A first look, or the last 15 minutes of class |
| Quick | 4 | ~18 min | ~10 min | A warm-up or a short block |
| Short | 6 | ~27 min | ~15 min | A shorter class with time to debrief |
| Half Period | 8 | ~35 min | ~19 min | A 45-minute class including setup and debrief |
| Standard | 10 | ~44 min | ~24 min | The full experience (a whole period, or a period plus a few minutes the next day) |
| Long | 14 | ~61 min | ~33 min | Two class days |
| Marathon | 20 | ~87 min | ~46 min | A multi-day project with more economic cycles and six ballot votes |
| Custom | 3–25 | | | Anything else |

Shorter games still include at least one Life/Economy Event per Year, at least one ballot vote, an Annual Report every Year, and the full Results page. Longer games show the business cycle repeating and let compound interest and loan payoffs play out.

## Suggested 45-minute lesson

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| **0–5 min: Setup** | Groups sit at one device. One student clicks **New Game**, types a group name (for example "Period 3 Table 2") and first names or nicknames, and leaves the empty seats as CPU players. Students take the 5-question **pre-check** (pass the device), then each picks a career (College, Trade Program or Straight to Work) and chooses to rent or buy. |
| **5–40 min: Play** | Standard length is 10 Years. A short tutorial runs on the first turn. Circulate and ask "Why did you choose that?" at decision points. At Years 3, 6 and 9 the group votes privately on a ballot measure. |
| **40–45 min: Debrief** | If time is short, open **⚙️ → End Game Now** (type END), which finishes the current turn and scores by net worth. On the Results page, students answer the reflection questions and take the **post-check**. Use **📋 Debrief Guide** for discussion questions about the events and votes that happened in their game. |

**Timing notes:**
- The simulator estimates a Standard game at about 44 minutes of play, plus 2–3 minutes for career picks (see `sim/REPORT.md`).
- If the setup and debrief must both fit in a single 45-minute period, choose one of these:
  - Use **Half Period (8 Years, ~35 min)** or **Short (6 Years, ~27 min)**.
  - Keep the **Year timer** on (default 4 minutes per Year) and plan to press **End Game Now** at the 40-minute mark.
  - Play over two class days. Games resume from the start screen on the same device.
- Turns per Year adjust to group size so the length stays about the same: 2 players get 4 turns each per Year, 3 players get 3, 4 players get 2, and 5–6 players alternate between 1 and 2.

## Before class

- Open the game on each device once to check that it loads. It works offline after the page has loaded.
- Pick settings on the setup screen:
  - **Game length.**
  - **Difficulty:** Easy has no taxes, credit or inflation; Standard is the default; Advanced adds depreciation and landlord-tenant Tenant Notices.
  - **Timers.**
  - **Quick Checks.**
  - **Ballot Measures.**
  - **Pre/Post check.**
- Optional: rename the streets after local places (see `README.md`). Students love landing on their own street.

## Classroom management tips

- **One driver, many voices.** One student holds the device while everyone discusses. Rotate the driver each turn: the game shows "Pass the device!" between turns.
- **Private votes are private.** On ballot measures, the game asks each student to take the device alone. Ask the group to look away.
- **The soft timer never plays for anyone.** After 45 seconds it shows "Hurry up!" only. The Year timer gives a gentle wrap-up warning and never cuts a turn short.
- **Struggling readers:**
  - The 🔊 button reads any card aloud.
  - **Why?** explains every choice.
  - Underlined words open a short definition.
  - The 📖 Glossary has 45+ terms.
- **Fast finishers:** have them open **🏦 Bank & Market → Loans** and compare a fixed and a variable loan, or open a player dashboard to read the full income statement and balance sheet.
- **Sound** is off with the 🔇 button. **Animation** can be set to Fast or Reduced in ⚙️ Settings, and the game follows the device's "reduce motion" setting.
- **Saving:** the game saves itself all the time. To continue another day, use the same device and browser account and press **Resume** next to the group name. Delete finished games from the start screen.
- **Collecting work:**
  - **Copy for Google Classroom/Canvas** copies each student's stats, awards, best and toughest decisions, and reflection answers as plain text, ready to paste.
  - **Download Game Log (CSV)** gives every move, transaction, event and vote by Year. It opens in Google Sheets with File → Import → Upload.
  - **Print / Save as PDF** prints the Results page.
- **Keep it fair:** CPU players follow simple strategies (residential, commercial, rail, saver, balanced). The game is part luck and part decisions, which is worth discussing!

## Big ideas to listen for

- *"My cash was high but my net worth was low."* That's the difference between **liquidity** and **wealth**.
- *"The recession hurt my shops more than my houses."* That's **commercial vs. residential risk** and the **business cycle**.
- *"My loan got more expensive after the Fed raised rates."* That's a **variable rate** and **monetary policy**.
- *"Insurance felt like a waste until the storm."* That's **risk management**: paying a small premium to avoid a big loss.
- *"We voted for the levy, and then my property tax went up."* Taxes and zoning are **political decisions** voters make, with tradeoffs.

The game is **neutral on every ballot measure**: each one shows a balanced "Supporters say / Opponents say" pair, and the game never suggests a "right" vote.

## Accuracy notes

Every card that simplifies real law or finance has a **Real world:** footnote. For example:
- Real mortgages are monthly loans lasting 15–30 years.
- A tax audit isn't jail.
- Student loans are rarely wiped out in bankruptcy.
- Landlord-tenant law varies by state.

Money amounts are scaled down to game dollars.
