## H. Mission 2 (Chain of Survival) findings
- **Drowning and the order of care.** The A-B-C vs. C-A-B handout was not received. Public sources: the AHA moved the sequence to C-A-B for adults, children and infants in 2010 (A-B-C stays for newborns), and the AAP/AHA 2024 drowning update says CPR **with rescue breaths** should begin before the AED is applied. The drowning and infant scenes therefore use 30 compressions then 2 breaths and never claim a "breaths first" rule. Question M2-04 asks which *kind* of CPR fits (with breaths), not what comes first.
- **Infant details** (two fingers, about 1.5 in deep, 30:2) come from the AHA 2020 pediatric guidance (search summary), not from your summary. The sim shows them; no scored question tests them. Tell me if you want infants simplified.
- **Chain of Survival wording.** M2-02 uses your answer-key wording (recognize, call 911, compress, use an AED, hand off to EMS). The AHA's own adult chain is worded differently (recognition and activation, CPR, defibrillation, advanced resuscitation, post-arrest care). Confirm yours is the one to grade.
- **Targets are visible in the sim.** The rate gauge shows a 100-120 zone and the depth bar a target band, because that is the technique being taught. No scored question asks for those numbers; M2-03 asks the student to compute a rate from a trace.
- **The AED trainer and M2-05.** A real AED states its steps in order, so the trainer inevitably practises the sequence that M2-05 asks students to order. The trainer only speaks after the student acts and tells the student when something is wrong, but the overlap is real. If you want, M2-05 can become a "what is wrong in this scene" item instead.
- **Dry chest.** The trainer says "It is dry" when the shirt is cut away; drying a wet chest is general AED practice, not from your materials. Infant AED use is not simulated.
- **Hands-free practice and the beat cue** are accessibility aids (no fine timing needed, no sound). Compressions made hands-free count toward scene completion exactly like manual ones.
