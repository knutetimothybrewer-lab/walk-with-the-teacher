# Teacher content notes: clarifications, corrections and assumptions

Rather than silently changing the curriculum, every departure from a slide or worksheet simplification is listed here.

## Missing source files
The six source files (five decks and the worksheet) were **not available** to the build. Content follows the curriculum specification in the project brief. Slide numbers in `docs/COVERAGE_MATRIX.md` are unverified, and no slide content was invented. Please compare the coverage matrix against your slides during review.

## Clarified adult ranges (Mission 2)
- **Blood pressure.** The slides give "below 120/80". The app adds the AHA categories (Normal, Elevated, Stage 1 category, Stage 2 category) and requires **both** numbers: for example 118/84 is a Stage 1 category because the bottom number is 80-89. It also stresses that one reading's category is **not a confirmed diagnosis.**
- **Resting heart rate 60-100.** Taught with context (fitness, caffeine, emotions, medicine). "Lower is not always better": a calm 54 in a well-trained adult with no symptoms is discussed, not condemned.
- **Fasting glucose "below 100".** Presented as NIDDK's normal classification (99 or below), prediabetes range 100-125, diabetes range 126+, always "needs confirmation." A one-sided limit does not mean every low value is safe.
- **Total cholesterol "below 200".** Adult (age 20+) reference only. The different reference for age 19 and under (below 170) is noted and never applied.
- **BMI 18.5-24.9.** Adult example only. Teens use age- and sex-specific BMI-for-age percentiles. BMI is a limited screening tool. There are no body measurements or weight-related goals anywhere, and no weight-loss promotion (fad-diet posts are fictional examples to critique).
- Cases are fictional **adults**. No student collects or enters personal health measurements.

## Worksheet scoring assumptions (Mission 6)
- All 20 categories and original weights are preserved: Physical (Sleep 3, Nutrition 2, Hydration 1, Movement 2), Mental (Screens 2, Stress 2, Focus 2, Rest 1), Emotional (Self-Talk 2, Expression 2, Setbacks 2, Gratitude 1), Social (Peer Pressure 3, Connection 2, Conflict 2, Kindness 1), Environmental (Space 1, Outdoors 1, Study/Work 2, Sustainable 1). A = +weight, B = 0, C = -weight. Daily range -35..+35; week -245..+245; domain weekly limits Physical +/-56, Mental +/-49, Emotional +/-49, Social +/-56, Environmental +/-35.
- These are **fictional teaching weights**, not a validated health score; points are never converted to assessment points.
- **Worksheet sleep (7-9 h) and movement (30 min) examples** are labeled "worksheet scoring rules" on the decisions. The app tells students CDC lists 8-10 hours for ages 13-17 and 60+ minutes of daily activity for ages 6-17 (30 minutes is a helpful step, not the full recommendation). Grading tests the model, not the thresholds as complete teen guidance.
- The worksheet suggestion that no single choice can cause serious harm is **not repeated as an absolute**. Some choices (such as riding with a driver who has been drinking) have immediate serious consequences, taught in Mission 5.
- Each of Jordan's 12 decisions changes exactly one category-day cell. The remaining 128 entries are scenario-provided defaults, all inspectable in the full log. There is no double counting. Cross-domain effects are shown as notes only.
- Scored Mission 6 questions use three **fixed** case weeks (Jordan, Maya, Eli) so they stay valid however the student plays the unscored week. If a student chooses all A, the baseline comparison remains.

## Habit research (Mission 3)
- Cue -> routine -> reward is taught as the **classroom habit model**, not a claim that all behavior works identically.
- The "21 days" claim is challenged using wide variation (Lally et al. 2009/2010; Singh et al. 2024). No exact statistic is graded and averages are not deadlines.
- The slide's 76% vs 43% goal-success figure is **omitted** (original study not verified). SMART is a planning framework, not a guarantee. SMART history is context only.

## Media literacy (Mission 4)
- All posts, accounts, products and study excerpts are **fictional and labeled** as such. Real guidance (CDC sleep range, NCCIH, NLM) is cited separately.
- Misinformation prevalence percentages from the slides are not reproduced. Evaluation skills are graded instead.
- A sales motive warrants scrutiny but does not alone prove falsity. AI generation and visual polish alone do not decide truth. Credentials need evidence. Reposts are not independent verification. Each of the five case types has a Credible, a Questionable and a Not Credible version.

## STOP (Mission 5)
- Exact acronym: **S** State the decision/problem, **T** Think of options, **O** Observe possible consequences, **P** Pick a responsible, health-enhancing choice and explain why.
- Multiple responsible choices are accepted. Personal responsibility does not mean perfect decisions or total control over resources, and **asking for support** is part of a responsible choice.
- No substance use is simulated as entertainment. Vaping and alcohol appear only as decisions to refuse or avoid.
- Scenario coverage: late-night gaming, skipping meals, school stress, scrolling, energy drinks vs water, responding after an argument, peer pressure to vape/use drugs, and a ride from someone who has been drinking.

## Branding and scope
- Neutral "Wildcats" branding is used because the decks name different schools. There is no gambling content.

## Other design choices to review
- Adjacent credibility ratings (for example Questionable vs Not Credible) earn half credit, and evidence credit depends on the rating chosen (mismatched evidence gets 0).
- Voluntarily "kept" scores can be reopened before final submission (attempt counts never reset).
- The optional reflection is never graded, never required, and is only included in the exported report if the student ticks "include my reflections."
- Optional practice (after submission) is unlimited, separate, and cannot change the grade.
