Health by a
Thousand Choices

Meet a person at age 14. You make the decisions. Fifty choices later they’ll be 65, and their body, mind, friendships, and surroundings will show the sum of everything you chose.

SState: read the situation
TThink: weigh the options
OObserve: predict what happens
PPick: then watch the result

Each choice becomes a habit that keeps working on the body as time passes, until the person faces a decision like it again. Watch the lungs, heart, skin, mood, and friendships change.

Your challenge: play at least two trials. In one, build the healthiest person possible. In the other, build the unhealthiest. You can play as many trials as you like. Then compare them.

A fictional, simplified model for classroom learning, not medical advice. If anything in this lesson feels heavy, talk with your teacher or school counselor.

Teacher notes

Runs offline. One file, no install. Open it in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari. Progress is saved only in each student’s own browser.

  • Lesson tie-in (STOP): every decision follows State → Think → Observe → Pick. After each pick the app shows what actually happened.
  • Worksheet tie-in: choices are scored on the same A (+) / B (0) / C (−) scale as Health by a Thousand Choices. The results screen shows the running-total graph, the Domain Balance Check (Physical, Mental, Emotional, Social, Environmental), and a full choice log students can copy into the Weekly Recap.
  • Time and habits: each choice sets a habit that keeps acting on the body as years pass (“chronic health or chronic hell”). Later choices in the same area can change course, because it is never too late.
  • Two-trial challenge: the title screen gives badges for completing a healthiest and an unhealthiest trial. The results page compares them side by side and offers reflection questions that mirror the worksheet.
  • Sensitive topics: vaping, alcohol, drugs, and depression appear with an emphasis on refusal skills and asking for help.
