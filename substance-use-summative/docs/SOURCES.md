# Sources and fact-check status

All externally sourced facts in the assessment come from U.S. government or public-health sources. When this was built, direct page access to cdc.gov, fda.gov, niaaa.nih.gov and nih.gov was blocked from the build environment, so facts were confirmed through search-result excerpts of the primary documents and from the builder's knowledge of the published tables. **"Confirmed"** means an excerpt of the primary source (or an authoritative secondary report of it) was seen during the build. **"Spot-check"** means the figure is a published value that was not re-read from the primary page; please confirm it against the linked source before relying on it for grading disputes. The question explanations deliberately avoid depending on the least certain digits (charts show rounded values and note it).

## Models (illustrative, not data)
* **BAC model** (`js/bacmodel.js`): Widmark-style distribution (r = 0.68), 14 g per drink, first-order absorption (slower with food), constant elimination 0.015 g/dL/h, with a plausible individual range (elimination 0.010 to 0.020, distribution factor +/-10%). A learning model only. It is never presented as a way to judge fitness to drive.
* **Reaction/stopping model** (`js/sims.js`): distance = speed x response time; braking distance = v squared over (2 x 0.7 x g); a fixed +300 ms "processing delay" for the modeled condition. A learning model only.
* **Cannabis route/timing chart** is an illustration, labeled as such.
* **Synapse animation** is a simplified particle model.

## Rules followed
No blogs, commercial rehabilitation sites, Wikipedia or unsourced statistics were used. Students are never asked to disclose personal or family substance use; all scenarios are fictional.
