# Teacher-facing content notes: accuracy and scope

Classroom sources set scope; where they simplify, these notes explain what the assessment says and why. Reference facts were checked only against search-result excerpts of the agency pages (direct page fetches were blocked); **verify each against the live page** (`docs/SOURCES.md`).

## Corrections and cautions built into the tasks
- **Healthcare vs public health.** Healthcare is not only treatment: preventive care (vaccines, screenings, check-ups) is healthcare. Public health is not only prevention: it also responds to existing threats (outbreak investigation, inspections). M2-U1 has a "both" category (a routine vaccination is individual preventive care that also contributes to community protection).
- **No "exactly 80%" claim.** The assessment never states that exactly 80% of outcomes arise from certain determinants. That figure is unsourced as a universal.
- **Transit is not a healthcare desert.** No task labels all places without transit as healthcare deserts. Tasks describe specific, sourced-in-case barriers (hourly bus, distance, clinic hours).
- **No promise of complete protection.** Vaccination and protective factors are described as reducing risk and contributing to community protection, never as guarantees. Risk factors are not destiny.
- **Agency responsibilities vary by jurisdiction.** M2-U4 asks for the *typical* match and says so. FDA oversight of most foods/drugs is noted in the explanation; roles of state vs local agencies differ by state.
- **Outbreak reasoning.** The cluster alone never establishes a cause. Students compare exposure rates (14/18 = 77.8% vs 1/12 = 8.3% for chicken salad) and still need lab/food-handling evidence.
- **AQI.** 101–150 is *Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups*; 151–200 is *Unhealthy*. AQI is not a personal dose; exertion, duration, pollutant and susceptibility matter. The 135–155 forecast deliberately spans both categories.
- **Noise.** NIOSH 85 dBA / 8 h with a 3 dBA exchange rate is an *occupational* recommendation. The assessment states it does not guarantee that anything below it is safe or that anything above it causes certain harm. The hearing-protection (−15 dB) and distance (−6 dB) adjustments in the explorer are *illustrative* values labeled as such; real fit and venues vary. **No audio is played.**
- **Heat.** Heat index is calculated for shade and light wind; sun can add substantially; sports risk also depends on exertion, equipment and acclimatization. No universal cancel threshold is invented ("only if the heat index reaches 125" is a distractor). The scenario value (98 °F) sits inside the "extreme caution" band under either edge listed in sources.
- **UV.** Guidance only (shade, cover, hat/sunglasses, SPF 30+ reapplied about every 2 hours, check the UV Index). No "time to burn" is given for any individual.
- **Water.** Boil-water ≠ do-not-drink ≠ do-not-use. Boiling kills germs but does not remove chemicals. Always follow the issuing authority's instructions.
- **Concept, not equation.** "Hazard × exposure × dose × duration" is presented as a *concept*; no formula multiplies AQI or UV Index by time into an illness probability. Exposure indicators in the lab are qualitative (Lower/Moderate/Higher/Highest) and labeled "not a medical risk score". The only calculation is the NIOSH time-halving rule used in M3-U4.
- **Disparities data.** Labeled "fictional classroom data", averages for groups. Tasks ask for differences/ratios, patterns without causation, and what is missing. M4-U3 explicitly asks what a community average tells about *one student* (nothing certain).
- **Marketing.** A technique does not make a claim false (M5-U2, M5-U4); popularity is not evidence. FTC: abbreviations like "sp" are not adequate disclosure. All ads, influencers and organizations are invented; no quotes are attributed to real agencies.
- **Source credibility.** A ".gov" label does not settle which source fits: the 2019 national survey loses to the current county survey for a local-prevalence question; a peer-reviewed study (different region) is best for a sleep-mechanism question; advocacy can be useful but a voluntary sample limits conclusions.
- **Advocacy.** The structured builder scores the *selected reasoning* (audience with power, feasible solution, accurate non-fearful message, realistic personal protection, evaluation of reach/understanding/action/improvement). It cannot assess the full range of an essay or oral pitch.
- **Asthma care.** Personal strategy options say "follow your own asthma action plan" and never give medical dosing advice.

## Privacy and tone choices
No task asks students to disclose diagnoses, substance use, addresses or other sensitive health information. All people and places are fictional. The assessment avoids blaming residents; deficit language is paired with community strengths.

## Known design limits
- Auto-grading uses selected options; open reflection is not graded and not collected.
- Distractors derive from common misconceptions, but only a pilot shows which options attract students; review `Summary` item accuracy and adjust.
- The noise chart labels the 100 dBA value, so M3-U4's numeric part tests reading plus the halving rule; consider removing labels above 94 dBA if you want more calculation.
